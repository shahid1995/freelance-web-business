# Client Discovery

**Status:** Phase 3 working delivery process  
**Owner:** Founder  
**Last updated:** 2026-09-27

## 1. Purpose

Discovery converts an accepted project handoff into a shared, evidence-based understanding of what will be delivered.

Discovery should confirm the client's goals, users, workflows, inputs, dependencies, constraints, decision owners, and acceptance expectations before detailed scope and milestone planning are finalized.

Discovery is a delivery activity. It does not replace sales qualification, the accepted commercial agreement, detailed scope definition, or the client's separate project repository.

## 2. Entry conditions

Begin discovery only after the opportunity has reached the delivery handoff boundary:

- the client has accepted the agreed commercial scope;
- the primary client contact is identified;
- the agreed service and major deliverables are known;
- required access and initial client inputs can be requested;
- the project has a dedicated client repository or approved project boundary.

The delivery owner should review the accepted proposal and any approved requirements before the first discovery interaction.

## 3. Discovery objectives

Discovery should establish enough shared understanding to answer:

1. What is being built, changed, or maintained?
2. Who will use it and in what important situations?
3. What existing content, systems, workflows, or assets must be preserved?
4. What constraints, dependencies, integrations, or approvals affect delivery?
5. What does the client consider a successful and acceptable deliverable?
6. Which questions remain unresolved before scope and milestones can be finalized?

Do not use discovery to silently add material work that was not accepted commercially.

## 4. Source hierarchy

Use the following sources in order:

1. Founder-approved business and delivery rules
2. Accepted proposal and approved commercial scope
3. Verified qualification and sales records
4. Approved discovery decisions and clarifications for questions they resolve
5. Client-provided requirements and project inputs that have not been superseded or clarified
6. Implementation detail created during delivery

An approved discovery decision or clarification supersedes an earlier client requirement for the specific point it resolves. It must not be used to silently expand or reduce the accepted commercial scope. When a discovery finding would materially change accepted scope, stop and record the difference before implementation and handle it through the agreed change-control process.

## 5. Discovery process

### Stage 1 — Handoff review

Review:

- accepted proposal;
- included and excluded work;
- deliverables and acceptance conditions;
- client responsibilities;
- assumptions and dependencies;
- timeline commitments, where approved;
- required integrations or external services;
- known risks and open questions.

Create an initial list of items that require confirmation.

### Stage 2 — Project context

Confirm the business context in plain language:

- project objective;
- desired user or customer outcome;
- current situation;
- reason the project is being undertaken now;
- important constraints;
- relevant existing systems or assets.

Separate stated facts from assumptions.

### Stage 3 — Users and workflows

Identify:

- primary user types;
- important user journeys;
- key tasks or workflows;
- permissions or role differences where relevant;
- important edge cases already known;
- existing workflows that must remain compatible.

Focus on the workflows required to define scope. Do not design every implementation detail during discovery.

### Stage 4 — Content, assets, and data

Confirm what the client will provide:

- written content;
- images and brand assets;
- existing site or application information;
- structured data;
- legal or compliance content;
- account and integration access;
- sample records or test data where appropriate.

Record ownership and expected delivery timing for material inputs.

### Stage 5 — Technical and operational dependencies

Identify, where relevant:

- third-party services;
- domain or hosting dependencies;
- analytics;
- authentication;
- email or notification services;
- payment or commerce integrations;
- data sources;
- browser/device requirements;
- security or privacy constraints;
- deployment or environment assumptions.

Do not promise compatibility or behavior that has not been verified.

### Stage 6 — Acceptance and decisions

Confirm how the client will recognize completion:

- required pages, screens, features, or workflows;
- critical user flows;
- content completeness;
- integration behavior;
- responsive expectations;
- agreed quality checks;
- handover expectations.

Record material decisions, unresolved questions, and who approved them.

### Stage 7 — Discovery sign-off

Before moving to detailed scope and milestones:

- summarize the agreed understanding;
- identify unresolved items;
- confirm client responsibilities;
- identify dependencies that could affect delivery;
- confirm any scope differences requiring change control;
- store the approved discovery record in the appropriate client project location.

## 6. Discovery questions

Use only questions needed to resolve actual uncertainty.

### Business context

- What should this project make easier, clearer, or possible?
- What is the current situation that the project is changing?
- Are there business, legal, operational, or launch constraints?

### Users and workflows

- Who are the primary users?
- What are the most important tasks they need to complete?
- Which workflows are essential at launch?
- Are there different roles or permissions?

### Existing material

- What existing pages, systems, content, or assets must remain?
- What should be replaced, migrated, or retired?
- What content or data still needs to be supplied?

### Integrations and dependencies

- Which external systems must connect to the solution?
- Who owns access and credentials?
- Are there third-party limits, approvals, or dependencies that could affect the project?

### Acceptance

- What will the client use to decide that the deliverable is complete?
- Which user flows or functions are most important to verify?
- What must be ready for handover?

## 7. Discovery outputs

A completed discovery process should produce:

- confirmed project objective;
- user and workflow summary;
- confirmed inputs and responsibilities;
- known integrations and dependencies;
- constraints and assumptions;
- acceptance expectations;
- decision log;
- unresolved questions;
- risks requiring attention;
- initial scope inputs for the next delivery stage.

Discovery should provide enough information to create or finalize a bounded scope and milestone plan.

## 8. Boundaries and change control

Discovery may clarify an existing requirement, but it must not silently expand the accepted project.

Treat these as material change candidates:

- new pages, screens, workflows, or features;
- new integrations;
- newly requested user roles or permission models;
- substantial content or migration work not previously included;
- materially different acceptance requirements;
- new environments, infrastructure, or operational responsibilities.

Material changes should be recorded and agreed before implementation proceeds.

## 9. Privacy and repository boundaries

Client-specific discovery records belong in the client's separate project repository or another explicitly approved operating location.

Do not place live client discovery records, private contact information, credentials, confidential datasets, private business documents, or meeting transcripts in this public business repository.

This document defines the reusable process only.

## 11. Discovery checklist

Before marking discovery Ready for scope:

- [ ] Accepted proposal has been reviewed.
- [ ] Project objective is understood.
- [ ] Primary users and important workflows are identified.
- [ ] Existing material and required inputs are identified.
- [ ] Material dependencies and integrations are recorded.
- [ ] Constraints and assumptions are visible.
- [ ] Acceptance expectations are understood.
- [ ] Decisions and unresolved questions are recorded.
- [ ] Client responsibilities are clear.
- [ ] Material scope differences are identified for change control.
- [ ] No confidential discovery material was added to the public business repository.

## 12. Handoff to scope

When discovery is Ready for scope:

1. Transfer confirmed requirements into the project scope.
2. Preserve explicit exclusions.
3. Convert material dependencies into milestone inputs.
4. Carry acceptance expectations into scope and QA planning.
5. Carry unresolved items forward rather than inventing answers.
6. Begin milestone planning only after the scope boundary is sufficiently clear.

The discovery record remains the evidence trail for how the delivery scope was understood.
