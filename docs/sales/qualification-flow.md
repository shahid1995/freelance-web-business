# Qualification Flow

**Status:** Phase 2 working qualification process  
**Owner:** Founder  
**Last updated:** 2026-09-27

## 1. Purpose

Qualification determines whether an incoming opportunity is sufficiently clear and appropriate to move toward a proposal.

The purpose is not to maximize the number of conversations. It is to protect scope clarity, delivery capacity, client fit, and the integrity of the sales process.

## 2. Qualification outcomes

Each opportunity should end in one of four states:

- **Qualified** — enough information exists to prepare a proposal or move to the next defined sales step.
- **Clarification required** — the opportunity may be relevant, but important information is missing.
- **Not a fit** — the requested work materially falls outside the current service boundaries, risk tolerance, capacity, or approved business direction.
- **No decision** — information is insufficient to determine fit and no further action is currently justified.

A qualification outcome is not a judgment about the client. It is a process state for the opportunity.

## 3. Qualification stages

### Stage 1 — Basic relevance

Confirm:

- What does the client need built, improved, or maintained?
- Which current service category appears relevant?
- What problem is the work intended to solve?
- Is the request related to the business's approved web-development scope?

Use the service catalog as the boundary.

### Stage 2 — Scope clarity

Determine:

- Main pages, screens, workflows, or functions required
- Known integrations or external systems
- Expected user types or audiences
- Existing assets or systems that must be retained
- Known exclusions or constraints
- Whether the request is a defined project or still exploratory

Do not attempt to solve every implementation detail before deciding whether the opportunity is qualified.

### Stage 3 — Client inputs and dependencies

Identify whether the client can provide the material inputs needed for the work, such as:

- content;
- brand assets;
- existing website access;
- required account/integration access;
- legal or compliance text;
- relevant business rules;
- timely feedback and approvals.

Record dependencies that could materially affect scope or delivery.

### Stage 4 — Timing and readiness

Understand:

- desired start timing;
- any business or launch deadline;
- whether required inputs are available;
- whether the client is ready to make decisions and provide feedback.

Do not promise a delivery date during qualification.

### Stage 5 — Decision process

Identify:

- who is evaluating the proposal;
- who can approve the scope;
- who will provide feedback;
- whether another stakeholder must be involved before work can begin.

This prevents avoidable proposal and approval confusion.

### Stage 6 — Commercial readiness

Determine whether the client is prepared to discuss commercial terms appropriate to the proposed scope.

Do not quote invented numbers.

Use approved pricing and commercial policy when those decisions are available. Until then, record the commercial requirement without creating a price.

### Stage 7 — Risk and boundary check

Check for material concerns such as:

- undefined or continuously expanding scope;
- requirements outside the approved service catalog;
- access to systems the client cannot authorize;
- sensitive data or security requirements that have not been understood;
- unrealistic deadlines dependent on unresolved inputs;
- requests to represent work, results, credentials, or experience inaccurately.

Escalate material ambiguity before proposal preparation.

## 4. Qualification questions

Use only the questions needed to resolve the current uncertainty.

### Problem

- What are you trying to build, change, or fix?
- What problem is this expected to solve?
- What would need to be true for the project to be considered complete?

### Scope

- Which pages, screens, features, or workflows are required?
- What already exists?
- What must remain unchanged?
- Are there known integrations or third-party services?

### Inputs

- Do you already have the content, brand assets, data, and account access required?
- Who will provide approvals and feedback?

### Timing

- When would you like to start?
- Is there a fixed external deadline?
- What dependencies could affect that date?

### Decision process

- Who needs to approve scope and commercial terms?
- Who will be the day-to-day contact?

### Commercial readiness

- Is there an approved commercial range or procurement requirement that should be understood before proposal preparation?

Do not turn the questionnaire into a generic form. Ask the minimum set that resolves the actual qualification gap.

## 5. Qualification decision rule

A lead is ready for proposal preparation when:

- the requested work maps to an approved service;
- the core problem is understood;
- the major deliverables are sufficiently clear;
- important dependencies are known;
- required client inputs are reasonably available or explicitly planned;
- the decision process is understood enough to proceed;
- commercial requirements can be handled using approved policy;
- no unresolved material risk blocks proposal preparation.

A lead does not need every implementation detail finalized before proposal preparation.

## 6. Stop or redirect conditions

Pause or redirect an opportunity when:

- the requested work is materially outside the approved services;
- the client cannot define the desired outcome well enough to establish scope;
- required access or inputs are unavailable and no workable path exists;
- security, privacy, legal, or operational risk is materially unclear;
- the requested deadline depends on conditions that are not under reasonable control;
- the client requests inaccurate representation of experience, results, ownership, or prior work;
- the opportunity requires a commercial policy that has not been approved.

Record the reason in the lead record without using unnecessary personal or confidential information.

## 8. Handoff to proposal

When qualified:

1. Transfer the verified requirements into the proposal structure.
2. Keep scope and exclusions aligned with the qualification record.
3. Use the relevant service-specific proposal prompts.
4. Add only approved commercial terms.
5. Review for unsupported claims.
6. Send only after internal review.

The qualification record remains the evidence trail for why the proposal scope was chosen.

## 9. Qualification checklist

Before marking an opportunity Qualified:

- [ ] Requested work maps to an approved service.
- [ ] Core problem is understood.
- [ ] Major deliverables are identifiable.
- [ ] Major dependencies are known.
- [ ] Required inputs are available or planned.
- [ ] Timing is understood without making an unsupported promise.
- [ ] Decision/approval path is understood.
- [ ] Commercial requirements are known enough to proceed under approved policy.
- [ ] Material risks are recorded.
- [ ] No inaccurate representation has been requested.
- [ ] Next action is clear.

## 10. Lifecycle

New opportunity → Relevance check → Scope clarification → Dependency check → Readiness check → Qualification outcome → Proposal or next action

Qualification should reduce ambiguity before commercial commitment, not replace discovery or the eventual project agreement.
