# Service Catalog

Phase 0.4 defines the initial public service catalog for the business.

The catalog is intentionally focused on six related service categories. Each service has a defined purpose, typical scope, boundaries, client inputs, optional extensions, and a practical completion definition.

Pricing is deliberately excluded from this catalog. Pricing is a separate Founder-owned business decision.

## Core services

| Service | Primary need | Typical output |
|---|---|---|
| [Business Website](business-website.md) | Establish or replace a professional business web presence | Responsive multi-page website |
| [Landing Page](landing-page.md) | Communicate one offer, audience, or action clearly | Focused conversion-oriented page |
| [Website Redesign / Modernization](website-redesign-modernization.md) | Improve an existing website without starting from zero | Redesigned or modernized website |
| [Custom Web Application](custom-web-application.md) | Provide functionality beyond a standard marketing website | Focused web application or workflow |
| [Dashboard / Portal](dashboard-portal.md) | Present operational information or user workflows in a structured interface | Role-appropriate dashboard or portal |
| [Website Maintenance / Improvements](website-maintenance-improvements.md) | Keep an existing website useful and improve it incrementally | Defined fixes, updates, and improvements |

## Catalog-wide scope rules

Every project should define the actual deliverables before implementation begins. A service category is a starting point, not permission for unlimited scope.

Unless explicitly included in a project scope, the business does not automatically provide:

- Copywriting or legal content creation
- Brand identity development
- Professional photography or video production
- Paid advertising management
- Guaranteed SEO rankings, leads, traffic, revenue, or conversions
- Third-party subscription fees
- Third-party licenses or paid API/data costs
- Ongoing content entry after the agreed handover
- Hosting, domain, or production operations outside the agreed responsibility
- Features or integrations discovered after scope approval

Technology choices are implementation details and may vary by project. The service definition describes the client-facing outcome, not a fixed technology stack.

## Standard client inputs

Depending on the service, the client may need to provide:

- Business name, description, and service information
- Brand assets and approved visual references
- Existing website access and relevant source material
- Page/content requirements
- User or customer workflow requirements
- Required integrations and third-party account access
- Legal, privacy, terms, or regulatory text where applicable
- Domain, hosting, analytics, or deployment details when the client owns those systems
- Timely feedback and approval

Missing or delayed inputs may change scope, schedule, or acceptance readiness and should be recorded.

## Standard completion principle

A service is complete when the agreed deliverables are implemented, the agreed acceptance criteria are met, known in-scope defects are addressed or documented, and the agreed handover material is provided.

A successful technical build alone does not expand the agreed scope or guarantee business outcomes.
