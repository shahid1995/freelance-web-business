# Audience and Problem Map

**Status:** Phase 0.3  
**Last updated:** 2026-09-27

| Audience | Typical situation | Primary web problem | Relevant service |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small/medium business | Website is outdated or missing | Credibility, clarity, mobile usability | Business website; redesign |
| Startup founder | Needs a credible launch presence | Limited time and incomplete web presence | Landing page; business website |
| Consultant/professional service | Offer is hard to explain online | Weak information architecture and conversion path | Business website; landing page |
| Agency | Has more work than internal capacity | Development bandwidth | Custom implementation; maintenance |
| SaaS founder/product team | Needs a focused web product surface | Product workflow or portal needs implementation | Custom web application; dashboard/portal |
| Existing website owner | Site works but needs ongoing changes | Maintenance backlog or modernization | Maintenance; improvements |

## Problem hierarchy

### Level 1 — Presence
The organization needs a professional web presence or a focused page for a specific offer.

### Level 2 — Clarity
The organization has a website, but users cannot quickly understand the business, offer, or next action.

### Level 3 — Experience
The site has usability, responsive, navigation, accessibility, performance, or consistency problems.

### Level 4 — Function
The organization needs functionality beyond a standard marketing site, such as a dashboard, portal, workflow, or custom application.

### Level 5 — Continuity
The organization has an existing web system that needs fixes, improvements, maintenance, or incremental modernization.

## Qualification principle

A lead is relevant when the requested work maps clearly to one or more of these problem levels and the expected deliverable can be defined before implementation begins.

The business should not force a lead into a service category when the underlying requirement is materially different.
