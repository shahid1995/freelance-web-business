# Business Positioning

**Status:** Phase 0.3 working position — Founder approval required for material public positioning  
**Last updated:** 2026-09-27

## 1. Positioning purpose

The business helps organizations turn outdated, unclear, or incomplete web experiences into modern, usable, maintainable websites and web applications.

The positioning is intentionally outcome-oriented: clients should understand what problem is being solved, what will be delivered, and why the work matters without needing to understand the internal development process.

## 2. Primary market

The initial market is organizations that need professional web work but do not need a large permanent engineering team for every project.

Priority audience groups:

1. Small and medium-sized businesses
2. Startup founders and early-stage companies
3. Consultants and professional-service businesses
4. Agencies that need reliable web-development capacity
5. SaaS founders and product teams needing focused web implementation
6. Businesses with outdated, confusing, slow, or poorly maintained websites

These groups are broad enough to support multiple acquisition channels while remaining centered on a common need: improving or building a business-critical web presence.

## 3. Core client problems

The business should speak to concrete problems rather than generic claims such as "we build great websites."

Common problems include:

- A business has no credible modern website.
- An existing website looks outdated or no longer represents the business.
- A landing page does not communicate the offer clearly.
- A website is difficult to use on mobile devices.
- Important pages or flows are missing or inconsistent.
- A business needs a custom portal, dashboard, or web application.
- Existing web work needs maintenance, fixes, improvements, or modernization.
- An agency or founder needs additional development capacity for a defined project.

## 4. Core service promise

The public promise is:

**Build, improve, and maintain practical web experiences that are clear to users, appropriate to the business, and delivered as defined.**

This is deliberately narrower than promising guaranteed business results. The business controls the quality and scope of the web deliverable; outcomes such as revenue, traffic, or conversion remain dependent on factors outside the development work.

## 5. Service categories

The positioning supports six public service categories:

- Business websites
- Landing pages
- Website redesign and modernization
- Custom web applications
- Dashboards and portals
- Website maintenance and improvements

Detailed scope, deliverables, exclusions, and pricing belong in the service and pricing documents rather than this positioning document.

## 6. Differentiation

The business should differentiate through observable delivery characteristics rather than unverifiable superlatives.

Public-facing differentiators:

- Clear scope and deliverables
- Modern, responsive web implementation
- Practical attention to usability and important user flows
- Maintainable implementation rather than one-off visual work only
- Appropriate attention to accessibility, performance, security, and integration concerns when relevant
- Structured handover and documentation appropriate to the project
- Honest representation of capabilities, experience, and results

These are standards the business can demonstrate through delivered work, portfolio evidence, documentation, and client communication.

## 7. What the business is not positioning itself as

The business is not initially positioned as:

- A full-service marketing agency
- A guaranteed SEO or lead-generation provider
- A branding agency
- A large software consultancy
- A provider of guaranteed revenue or conversion outcomes
- A low-cost "anything for anyone" development service

Specialized services can be added later if there is evidence of demand and the Founder approves the change.

## 8. Positioning statement

### Internal positioning statement

**A professional web-development business for organizations that need to build, modernize, or maintain a reliable web presence or focused web application without committing to a large permanent engineering team.**

### Short public description

**We build, modernize, and maintain business websites and web applications with clear scope, practical usability, and maintainable implementation.**

## 9. Positioning principles

All public positioning should:

1. Lead with client problems and useful outcomes.
2. Describe services in plain language.
3. Avoid claims that cannot be demonstrated.
4. Separate development deliverables from business outcomes the business cannot guarantee.
5. Keep the initial market broad enough for acquisition while maintaining a coherent service identity.
6. Evolve based on evidence from real projects, qualified leads, client feedback, and portfolio performance.

## 10. Approval boundary

This document establishes a structured positioning proposal for Phase 0.3. Material changes to target market, public claims, or core positioning require Founder approval before being treated as final business policy.
